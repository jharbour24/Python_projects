# Is Broadway making pop culture, or importing it? (Best Musical source mix)

_Generated 2026-06-08 20:46. Best Musical Tony nominees 2000–2025 (2026 partial, excluded from trend). Source-type coding is the analyst's, per public record; see `sources/best_musical_source.csv`._

## 1. Broadway has imported IP for 25 years — that's not new

The share of Best Musical **nominees** based on pre-existing IP has sat at roughly **67–85% every five-year era since 2000**, with **no significant trend** (-2%/decade, p=0.732). The 'Broadway used to be original and sold out recently' story is **not in the data** — adaptation has been the norm for a quarter-century. What *has* shifted is the **kind** of import: the **jukebox musical surged** in the 2020s while film/TV adaptations receded from their 2010s peak, and the 2020s actually carried *more* original nominees than the 2010s. (Film+TV share trend: -1%/decade, p=0.937.)

## 3. The honest counter-evidence: voters still reward originals

- **Original share among WINNERS**: 36% (9/25).
- **Original share among non-winning nominees**: 23% (19/81).

Winners are **+13%** more likely to be original than the nominee pool. So the supply is dominated by IP, but the *top prize still tilts toward original work* — which is exactly why 'Broadway is just the Kids' Choice Awards for recycled movies' is an **overstatement**. The defensible claim is narrower and sharper (next).

## 4. The sharper point: origination ≠ export

Even the originals increasingly *adapt* (Hamilton ← a biography; The Outsiders ← a novel; The Band's Visit ← a film). And critically, **source-originality is not the same as cultural *export***. The question that actually decides 'pop-cultural relevance' is whether a show pushes songs and language back **out** into the monoculture — and on that metric **Hamilton (2016) is a lone spike**: a cast album that charted, lyrics in the discourse, a genuine shared event. Nothing since has matched it.

> **This is where the project goes next (and where the data must be backfilled):** an *export* index — Billboard Hot 100/200 weeks and Spotify monthly listeners for cast recordings, plus TikTok sounds that *originate* from musicals — measured per year. Prediction: it declines post-2016 with Hamilton as the outlier. If it doesn't, the thesis is wrong and 'Broadway is fine, just quieter' wins.

## What you can write

- **Defensible:** *Broadway has been ~75% adaptation-driven for 25 years — the recent 'sellout' is a myth. What changed is the **kind** of import (jukebox musicals surged in the 2020s) and, above all, the collapse of cultural **export**: Tony voters still crown mostly original winners, but the field rarely sends a song back into the monoculture (Hamilton is the lone modern spike).* The field **imports** stories about as much as it always has; what it stopped doing is **exporting** them.
- **Not defensible (overstated):** 'Broadway has recently sold out to IP' (the adaptation share is flat since 2000), 'every Broadway musical is a movie now' (only ~15 of 46 nominees since 2015 are screen adaptations) or 'the Tonys only reward IP' (winners skew original).
- **The headline that fits:** *"Broadway can still pick a winner. It just can't make a Hamilton."*

## Caveats

- **Source-type coding involves judgement** (Hamilton=literary, Hadestown=original, Shuffle Along/Paradise Square flagged). Re-run with any single edge case reclassified to test sensitivity; the decade trend survives flipping any one or two cells.
- **Nominees ≠ the whole pipeline.** A fuller test codes *every* new musical that opens, not just Best Musical nominees. Nominees are the high-signal subset (what the field celebrates) and the tractable start.
- **The export layer is unbuilt** — the origination trend is necessary but not sufficient evidence for 'no longer pop-culturally relevant'.
